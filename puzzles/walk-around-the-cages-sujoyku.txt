tip Each of the four 9-cell cages has no total, so it is just a 1-9 set (digits don't repeat). An arrow with two shafts is a fork: each shaft sums to the circle on its own, not added together. So r1c6 equals both r1c5+r1c4 and r2c6+r3c6, and r7c4 equals both r6c4+r5c4 and r7c3+r7c2. Every shaft in this puzzle is two cells, so each circle is 3-9 (a 1 or 2 cannot be the sum of two positive digits of at most 9).
tip The forked arrow at r7c4 sits entirely inside the lower-left cage (r5c2, r5c3, r5c4, r6c2, r6c3, r6c4, r7c2, r7c3, r7c4). Those five arrow cells are five different digits, and the two 2-cell shafts each sum to r7c4. A 3 or 4 has at most one pair of different digits that add up to it, so r7c4 is 5-9, and the shafts are two disjoint pairs from the pairs of 1-8 that sum to that circle.
tip The other fork has its circle at r1c6, one shaft along r1c5-r1c4, and one down r2c6-r3c6; r3c6 is in the upper-right cage. The upper-left cage contains a V at r2c3-r3c3 (so 14 or 23) and an X at r4c3-r4c4 (19, 28, 37 or 46); those four cells are four different digits. Column 1 has a white (consecutive) dot at r3c1-r4c1, an X at r4c1-r4c2, another X at r5c1-r6c1, and a second white dot at r7c1-r8c1.
