tip Column 9 is almost all green whispers — the top-right corner, the 3-cell line on the right edge, and the bottom-right corner. That leaves only r3c9 and r7c9 as places for a 5. Box 9 already has the given 5 at r8c8, so r3c9 is 5. The white dot then makes r4c9 a 4 or a 6, and the green line below it has r5c9 as 9 or 1.
tip The given 5 also has a white dot to r8c9, so r8c9 is 4 or 6, and the corner whisper forces r9c9 to be 9 or 1. In the two 2×2 corners that have both a pink L and a green L (top-right and bottom-left), the two cells that sit on both lines are the ends of the whisper, so they have the same polarity (both 1–4 or both 6–9) and cannot be 5. The 3-cell renban is then 123, 234 or 345 (both low) or 567, 678 or 789 (both high), and a 5 on that renban can only go in the cell that is not on the whisper.
tip Next look at the downward arrow in column 7 (circle in r7c7, shaft up through r6c7–r4c7). The three shaft digits are distinct and sit in the same column as the circle, so the circle is 6–9. That arrow rules out r8c9 = 4 (which would force r9c9 = 9, r4c9 = 6 and r5c9 = 1), so r8c9 is 6 and r9c9 is 1. The same arrow also forces the shared cells of those pink/green corners to be low (1–4).
